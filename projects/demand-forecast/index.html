Demand forecasting and replenishment

I clean three years of messy weekly sales, forecast each SKU with Holt-Winters written from scratch, score it on weeks the model never saw, and turn the forecast into a reorder point.

This is a personal demo project. It runs in your browser on sample data generated from a seeded random number generator. It is not Alo Yoga's code or data.

    How it works

    The page generates 40 SKUs with 156 weeks of unit sales each. Every SKU has its own level, trend, yearly season, promo spikes and noise, and a few are slow sellers with many zero weeks. Then it damages the data on purpose: duplicated rows, missing weeks, negative quantities and a few extreme values. The pipeline removes duplicates, fills gaps, flips sign errors and caps outliers. Every cleaning rule only looks backwards in time or stays inside its own side of the train and holdout split, so nothing from the holdout leaks into training.

    The forecast is additive Holt-Winters with a 52-week season. It keeps three numbers up to date each week: the level, the trend, and a seasonal offset for that week of the year. Three weights control how fast each one reacts. I pick the weights per SKU with a small grid search: fit on weeks 1 to 104, forecast weeks 105 to 130, keep the weights with the lowest error. The last 26 weeks play no part in that choice. The grid is kept small and slow-moving on purpose, because one validation slice is easy to overfit.

    Scoring on a holdout matters because a model always looks good on the data it was fitted to. The baseline is seasonal naive, which repeats the same week from last year. It is hard to beat on seasonal retail data, so it is the right thing to compare against. I use WAPE, total absolute error divided by total units, instead of MAPE. MAPE divides by each week's actual, so it breaks on zero weeks and lets slow sellers with tiny volumes dominate the average. WAPE stays defined and weights errors by the units that matter. Holdout weeks that had to be imputed are left out of the score.

    For replenishment, safety stock is z × σ × √lead time, where σ is the standard deviation of the holdout forecast errors and z comes from the service level. The reorder point is forecast demand over the lead time plus safety stock. The suggested order tops stock up to cover lead time plus one order cycle.

    What this shows

    I can take raw sales data from messy rows to a stocking decision: clean it with stated rules, forecast it with a method I understand well enough to write myself, and judge it against a simple baseline on data the model has not seen. Where the baseline wins on a SKU, the table says so. The data is generated, so the numbers show the method working, not a result from any real business.
