Concurrent price upload

I built this to show how much faster a bulk price upload gets when you run it concurrently, without flooding the API. It uploads prices for 12 markets twice, once one batch at a time and once through a worker pool limited by a semaphore, so you can compare the two.

Personal project, built on my own time. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or based on the code or data of any employer. Everything runs in your browser on sample data generated from a seeded random number generator, and all stores, products and figures are invented. There is no real API: every latency and time on this page is simulated.

How it works

Each market has a queue of price batches of up to SKUs. The sequential uploader sends one batch, waits for the response, then sends the next. The concurrent uploader starts a few workers per market. Every worker must take a permit from one shared semaphore before it calls the API, and gives it back when the call ends.

I use a semaphore instead of firing every request at once with Promise.all because a pricing API has rate limits. Unbounded requests turn into a wall of 429 responses and timeouts. A fixed number of permits gives back-pressure: when the API slows down, permits come back slower, and the uploader slows down with it. Waiters are served first in, first out, so no worker is skipped.

The per-market limit is the number of workers each market gets. Without it, the market with the most batches could hold most of the permits and the small markets would wait behind it. With it, the big market finishes last but never blocks the others.

A small share of simulated calls fail with a 429 or a timeout. The worker keeps its permit, waits with exponential backoff, and tries again, up to retries. What this shows

I can take a slow, one-at-a-time integration and make it concurrent without overloading the system on the other side: bounded concurrency, fairness between markets, and retries that respect rate limits. It is a general pattern I wrote from scratch to explain the idea, with no connection to any employer's systems.
