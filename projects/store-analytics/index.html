How it works

The demo starts by generating the data. A seeded generator creates 12 stores in 4 regions and 5 categories, then writes one row per store, category and week with traffic, transactions, units, net sales, opening inventory and receipts. I gave the data the shape real store data has: stores of different sizes, a seasonal curve for each category, a holiday peak, and random noise on top.

Each headline figure is a ratio of sums over the filtered rows, not an average of averages. Conversion is transactions divided by traffic. Sell-through is units sold divided by opening inventory plus receipts. Weeks of supply is closing inventory divided by average weekly units. The change under each figure compares against the period of the same length just before.

One category gets a four-week promotion in half the stores. I paired stores by size and put one of each pair in the promotion, so the two groups are comparable. The promotion raises units by a fixed amount that the analysis is not told. Difference-in-differences then takes the change in the promo stores and subtracts the change in the control stores over the same weeks. That removes the seasonal movement both groups share and leaves the effect of the promotion.

What this shows

I can take raw store rows and turn them into the figures a retail team uses to decide things: which stores convert, where stock is sitting too long, and whether a promotion paid for itself. I also check my own method. The data has a known answer built in, so the page shows whether the analysis finds it instead of asking you to trust it.
